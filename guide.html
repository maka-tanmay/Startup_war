Four friends. Five stages. One decision.

SparkTank turns a casual idea night into a fair decision session: everyone drafts, everyone reviews the business, every idea gets presented, and the group compares its scoreboard with an independent LLM Council.

Is that what the app does?

Yes—after the latest update. “Battle” is now Review, each friend’s rating is stored separately, rooms keep their own history, presentation order is randomized, council reviews have their own show button, and Results is a proper scoreboard.

Your rooms stay in history

A room is one complete decision session. Room 1 and Room 2 never share people, ideas, ratings, presentation progress, or council output.

1→2

Create Room 2 without erasing Room 1

Open Lobby, click New room, and add the next group. To return later, use the Saved room dropdown and choose Room 1. Local preview saves rooms in this browser; a Supabase deployment saves and syncs them for the shared group.

Run the room in order

One person can host on a shared screen while each friend uses their own laptop. The top navigation always shows the current stage, but the group should finish each stage before moving on.

01

Lobby

Add exactly four friends and claim your own seat.

  • Use recognizable names.
  • Each person selects their name before drafting or rating.
  • The active seat controls who owns the next action.
5 min
02

Draft

Turn rough thoughts into comparable startup concepts.

  • Give each idea a title and one-line pitch.
  • Complete the problem, customer, revenue, cost, launch, market, advantage, risk, and notes.
  • Friends can draft more than one idea.
15 min
03

Review

This replaces the confusing “Battle” stage.

  • Review the idea—not presentation skill or friendship.
  • Each friend saves one rating per idea and can update it.
  • Ratings stay separate, then become a group average.
10 min
04

Present

The app creates a neutral, randomized idea order.

  • Select the next idea from the On stage dropdown.
  • The presenter sees every draft field on one page.
  • Mark each presentation complete; council judgment stays locked until all are heard.
15 min
05

Scoreboard

Compare the group’s ratings, then make the decision yourselves.

  • Rank by the four-criterion average.
  • Open any idea for details and comments.
  • Keep the human winner and council recommendation visibly separate.
5 min

What the 1–5 ratings mean

Use evidence from the idea draft. A polished speaker should not receive extra points; the presentation exists to make the concept understandable before the final decision.

CriterionQuestion to answerA high score means
Problem valueIs the pain real, specific, and urgent?People already feel the problem and have reason to act.
Market potentialCan this reach worthwhile customer demand?The customer is identifiable and the opportunity can grow.
DifferentiationDoes the idea have a defendable edge?It is meaningfully better, clearer, or harder to copy.
FeasibilityCan a small team launch and learn?The first version is realistic in time, cost, and complexity.

Two opinions, one decision

The council is a second lens—not an automatic winner picker. It never changes the human scoreboard, and it only becomes available after every presentation is complete.

If something looks wrong

The app works locally without Supabase, but shared multi-laptop sessions and real council runs need their services configured.

The four laptops do not show the same roster, rooms, or ratings.

Use the deployed app with Supabase configured and apply the rooms, presentation-council, and idea-ratings migrations. Local preview mode stores room history only in that browser.

The council review does not become ready.

For local use, start SparkTank with npm run dev so the Vite council bridge can call the authenticated Codex CLI in the background. A deployed browser needs a reachable runner configured through VITE_COUNCIL_ENDPOINT.

The scoreboard has fewer than four ratings.

Each friend must claim their own seat, open Review, and save one rating for that idea. Updating a rating replaces only that friend’s previous rating.
